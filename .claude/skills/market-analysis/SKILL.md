# Market analysis (CMA)

## 1. Inputs
- **Subject property:** address, beds, baths, square footage, lot, year built, condition, and notable features or defects.
- **Comparable sales:** ideally 3–6 sold within the last 6 months, about a mile away (closer in cities), and within ±20% of the subject's square footage. Also use active and pending listings for context.
- If the agent gives no comps, ask for them. **Never invent sales data or prices.**

## 2. Analyze
1. Put the comps in a table with address, status, sold or list price, sold date, beds/baths, square feet, price per square foot, days on market, and key differences.
2. Adjust each sold comp toward the subject for the main differences: square footage, beds and baths, garage, pool, condition or updates, lot, and view. State every adjustment amount and why. If the agent has local adjustment values, use theirs; otherwise label yours as estimates.
3. Compute the adjusted price range, the median price per square foot, and average days on market.
4. Use active listings as competition and pending listings as the direction of the market. Give the most weight to closed sales.

## 3. Output
- **Recommended list price range** and a single suggested list price, with the reasoning in 2–3 sentences.
- The adjusted comp table.
- **Market snapshot:** the trend direction, months of inventory if the data allows, and sale-to-list ratio.
- **Pricing strategy:** expected days on market at each price point and the risk of overpricing.
- **Seller-friendly summary:** a short, plain-English paragraph the agent can use in the listing appointment.

If the agent wants a spreadsheet or file, produce one.

## 4. Rules
- Always state that this is a broker price opinion or CMA, **not an appraisal**.
- Show your math. Flag weak comps (too old, too far, too different) and say how confident you are.
- Don't fold in neighborhood demographics. Stick to property and market data.
