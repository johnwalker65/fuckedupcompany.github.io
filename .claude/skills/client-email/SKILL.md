# Client email and text

## 1. Pin down the situation
Work out (or ask briefly) the following:
- **Who:** buyer, seller, new lead, past client, cooperating agent, lender, or title company
- **Stage:** new lead, active search, showing, offer, under contract, closing, or post-close
- **Goal:** the one action you want the reader to take
- **Channel:** email or text. Default to email.
- **Facts** to include, such as dates, prices, addresses and deadlines

## 2. Draft
- **Email:** a subject line under 50 characters, a greeting with their first name, at most 3 short paragraphs, one clear call to action, and a sign-off with `[Agent Name]` and `[Phone]` placeholders.
- **Text:** 1–3 sentences, friendly, with no subject line and no emojis unless the agent uses them.
- Offer a second, shorter or warmer version when the tone is uncertain.

## 3. Playbook by situation
- **New lead:** reply fast and personal, answer their question, ask one qualifying question (timeline, pre-approval, or area), and suggest a call time.
- **After showing:** recap what they liked, ask for a 1–10 rating, and suggest the next step.
- **Offer update:** put facts first (accepted, countered, or rejected), then the deadlines and what you need from them.
- **Price reduction talk (seller):** lead with market data, such as days on market, showings, feedback and comparable homes. Be empathetic, never blame, and give a specific recommendation.
- **Under contract:** list the upcoming dates (inspection, appraisal, financing, closing) and who does what.
- **Past client or referral:** keep it personal and low-pressure, add value (home value update, market note), and make the referral ask light.

## 4. Rules
- Never promise outcomes ("you'll definitely get it", "it will appraise").
- Never share the other party's confidential information, such as a seller's motivation or a buyer's maximum price.
- Leave legal, tax, and lending advice to the professionals, and suggest the client ask their attorney, CPA, or lender.
- Mark facts you are unsure of as `[confirm]`.
