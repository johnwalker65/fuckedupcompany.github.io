# Offer comparison

## 1. Collect terms for each offer
Price, earnest money, down payment and loan type (cash, conventional, FHA, VA), financing, inspection and appraisal contingencies with deadlines, appraisal gap coverage, sale-of-home contingency, closing date, seller concessions or credits, rent-back, escalation clause (increment and cap), items included or excluded, and pre-approval or proof of funds.

Ask for missing items. Don't guess.

## 2. Compare
1. Build a side-by-side table with one column per offer and one row per term.
2. **Estimate net proceeds** for each offer: price, minus concessions, commissions, the seller's closing costs, mortgage payoff, and other costs the agent provides. Show the math. If the agent didn't supply a cost, list it as `[confirm]`.
3. **Assess risk** for each offer as low, medium, or high, with reasons: financing strength, contingencies, appraisal exposure (low down payment plus a high price), and timing fit with the seller's plans.
4. If an escalation clause applies, work out the escalated price and confirm the conditions for proof.

## 3. Output
- The comparison table and net sheet.
- **The strongest offer on net and on certainty**, which may not be the same offer.
- Options to present to the seller: accept, counter one offer, or call for highest and best from all buyers.
- A plain-English summary the agent can walk the seller through.

## 4. Rules
- The seller decides. Give options and tradeoffs, not a single directive.
- Net figures are estimates. The agent should confirm them with title, escrow, or an attorney.
- Treat every offer's terms as confidential. Don't draft messages that reveal one buyer's terms to another unless the seller has authorized it and local rules allow it.
- Don't give legal or tax advice. Point the seller to their attorney or CPA.
