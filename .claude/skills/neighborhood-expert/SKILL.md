# Neighborhood expert

## 1. Scope the request
Ask or infer:
- **Area:** a specific address, or a neighborhood, city, or ZIP code, plus the boundaries if they're unclear
- **Purpose:** quick brief before a showing, buyer consultation, relocation packet, listing marketing, social post or blog, or a comparison of 2–4 areas
- **The buyer's stated criteria**, if any: budget, commute destination, home type, lot size, and must-have features. Use only what the client asked for.

## 2. Gather facts with sources
- If web search is available, use it and **cite a source and date for every number**. Good sources are the agent's MLS data, city and county websites, the census (housing data only), school district websites, the state education department, transit agencies, and park districts.
- If you can't verify something, mark it `[confirm]`. **Never invent statistics**, prices, school ratings, or business names.
- Prefer data the agent supplies (MLS exports, local knowledge) over general web data.

## 3. Guide structure
1. **Snapshot:** 2–3 sentences on the character of the place: architecture, layout, and pace (walkable, suburban, rural).
2. **Housing:** common home types and eras, median sale price and its trend, typical days on market, price per square foot, HOA presence, and the mix of new construction and older homes.
3. **Getting around:** commute times to the main job centers, transit lines, highways, airport distance, walkability, and bike paths.
4. **Schools:** assigned districts and schools, with a link to the official district boundary lookup. Give factual information only (names, grades, programs offered) and link the official state report cards. **Do not rank schools as "good" or "bad".** Tell buyers to verify the assignment for a specific address with the district.
5. **Parks and recreation:** parks, trails, water, gyms, and community centers.
6. **Food, shopping and errands:** grocery stores, the main shopping areas, and well-known local restaurants or cafés (only ones you can verify).
7. **Things to do:** annual events, farmers markets, arts and venues.
8. **Costs to know:** property tax rate, special assessments, utilities, and flood zone or insurance considerations, each with a source.
9. **Agent tips:** what moves fast, micro-pockets, and recent developments. Only include these if the agent provides them or a source confirms them.

10. **Nearby sales:** for an address, list 3–5 recent nearby sales (address, price, beds and baths, square feet, sold date, price per square foot) from the agent's data or a reliable source, and say how the subject compares. If solid data isn't available, don't guess. Give the exact MLS search to run instead (radius, date range, size range, property type).
11. **Pros and cons:**
    - **Top 3 strengths**, each with a specific fact.
    - **Top 3 things that might give a buyer pause**, each with a source: flood zone, highway, rail or airport noise, a long commute, a high tax rate, a pending special assessment, nearby construction, a limited walkable retail area. An agent's credibility comes from honesty, not cheerleading.
    - **Which property and lifestyle needs it fits or doesn't**, such as commute, yard size, walkability, budget or home style. Never frame this around who the buyer is.
12. **Talking points:** 5 ready-to-say lines for a showing or consultation. Each one is a concrete fact, not a generic compliment, and together they include at least one number and one lifestyle detail.

For **comparisons**, give a side-by-side table across these categories, then a neutral summary of the tradeoffs in light of the client's stated criteria.

## 4. Fair housing rules (required)
Neighborhood content carries the highest risk of steering, so follow these strictly:
- **Never describe or compare areas by who lives there:** race, ethnicity, religion, national origin, familial status ("family neighborhood", "lots of young professionals"), disability, sex, or any other protected class. Don't cite demographic breakdowns.
- **Don't judge safety.** Avoid "safe", "dangerous", "good area", "bad area", "up-and-coming", "transitional", and "exclusive". If a client asks about crime, refer them to the local police department's public crime maps and let them decide.
- **Don't steer.** Present options based on the client's own criteria, and never suggest an area is or isn't "right for" someone because of who they are.
- Describe **places, features, and facts**, not people.

## 5. Output formats
- **Quick brief** (the default when given an address): headers and short bullets the agent can read on a phone between showings. Cover the snapshot, housing and prices, schools, getting around, nearby places, nearby sales, pros and cons, and talking points.
- **Buyer packet:** the full guide, about 1–2 pages, with a source list at the end.
- **Social or blog post:** an engaging "living in [area]" piece. Pair it with the social-media-post skill for captions.
- **Quick facts card:** 8–10 bullet facts for a flyer or open house.
- **Comparison table:** for areas the client is deciding between.

If the agent wants a file, produce one. End every guide with the line: "Information deemed reliable but not guaranteed; verify independently."
